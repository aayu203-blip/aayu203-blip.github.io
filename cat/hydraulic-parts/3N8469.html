Failure Symptoms

The first indication of a failing auxiliary seawater pump is often a rise in engine coolant temperature, especially under load. If left unaddressed, the pump may cease to circulate water altogether, leading to severe engine overheating and potential component damage. Continued operation without adequate cooling risks significant engine downtime and costly repairs.

Installation Tip

Ensure the engine is completely cool before starting disassembly to avoid burns from residual heat. Do not reuse the old gasket; always install a new one to ensure a proper seal and prevent leaks.

Service Note

Inspect the engine's heat exchanger for scale buildup or leaks, and check the condition of all coolant hoses and clamps.
