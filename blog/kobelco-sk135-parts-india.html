Kobelco SK135 — The Compact Excavator for Urban India

The Kobelco SK135SR (Super Reduced tail swing) has built a strong following in Indian urban construction markets where the ZX135US, Komatsu PC138US, and Hyundai R140LC-9 compete for the same confined-site work. At 13.5 tonnes, the SK135SR delivers near-14-tonne performance with zero-tail-swing geometry — enabling work in trenches, inside buildings, and alongside live traffic lanes without the tail swing clearance concerns of conventional excavators.

The SK135SR-2 and SK135SR-3 are the most common variants in Indian service. Both use the Hino J05E engine — a reliable 5.0-litre four-cylinder turbocharged common-rail unit also found in the larger Kobelco SK210-10.

SK135SR-2 and SK135SR-3 — Key Differences

Kobelco SK135SR-2 (2007–2014)

The SK135SR-2 uses the Hino J05E-TA engine producing 74 kW (99 hp). The hydraulic pump is a Kawasaki K3V63 — the same platform as the Komatsu PC138. The SK135SR-2 introduced Kobelco's iNDr cooling system in the small excavator class, reducing engine compartment heat and noise. Most cab and body components are specific to the SR-2.

Kobelco SK135SR-3 (2014–Present)

The SK135SR-3 meets Tier 4 Interim / Stage IIIB requirements with a refined J05E-TA engine producing 75 kW (100 hp). The hydraulic system was updated with improved HIOS V control for better multi-function efficiency. The cab received a redesigned monitor. Engine and hydraulic pump parts are largely shared with the SR-2; cab and body components differ.

SK135SR Common Issues — Urban Cycle Patterns

  • Slewing bearing wear — Urban SK135 machines cycle the swing constantly in trench work. Measure slewing bearing play (should not exceed 1.5 mm) at every annual service. Replace when play exceeds 3 mm.
  • Boom and arm pin wear — High-cycle urban work accelerates boom pin and bushing wear. Grease all pivot points every 50 hours; replace pins when diametral wear exceeds 1.5 mm.
  • Common-rail injector contamination — Hino J05E common-rail system is sensitive to fuel contamination. Use quality secondary fuel filters; change at 500-hour intervals.
  • iNDr partition seal degradation — Torn cooling partition seals cause rapid hydraulic oil overheating. Inspect at every 500-hour service.
