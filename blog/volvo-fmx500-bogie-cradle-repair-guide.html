What Is the Volvo FMX500 Bogie Rear Axle?

On a standard 4×2 or 6×2 truck, the rear axle is fixed to the chassis — it goes where the frame goes. The Volvo FMX 6×4 uses a fundamentally different rear suspension architecture: the bogie tandem rear axle. Two drive axles are physically connected by a steel beam called the bogie cradle, which pivots on a central chassis bracket. As the truck crosses uneven ground, each axle can rise or fall independently, keeping all four rear wheels on the ground and distributing load equally across both drive axles.

This is what makes the FMX exceptional in construction, quarry, and off-highway applications. On a site road with a severe rut, a standard truck would lift one rear wheel entirely, losing traction and potentially overloading the remaining axle. The FMX bogie maintains contact on both sides regardless of the surface irregularity.

The bogie system has two key serviceable components at its pivot point:

  • Part 22717828 — Bogie Cradle Bushing: The rubber-bonded steel bush at the pivot point. Wears out over time. Normal replacement item at 300,000 km intervals.
  • Part 22913290 — Bogie Cradle Assembly: The fabricated steel beam itself. Designed to be a life-of-vehicle component. Replacement triggered by structural damage.

Understanding which of these two components is at fault is the critical diagnostic step — and it determines whether you have a relatively straightforward bushing replacement or a significant structural job on your hands.

The Two Parts: What They Do and How They Differ

22717828 — Bogie Cradle Bushing

The bogie pivot bushing is a three-piece rubber-bonded metal component: a steel outer sleeve pressed into the cradle beam's pivot eye, a steel inner sleeve that the pivot bolt passes through, and vulcanized rubber bonded between them. The rubber provides compliance in all directions, absorbing torsional and vertical loads, while the steel handles structural load transfer.

The rubber bond fatigues over time and with use — especially under the sustained torsional cycling the FMX bogie generates on rough terrain and under full load. It is a wear item, the same way tyres and brake pads are wear items. Typical service life: 300,000–500,000 km on-highway; 150,000–200,000 km in heavy construction use. Always replace in pairs — LH and RH simultaneously.

22913290 — Bogie Cradle Assembly

The bogie cradle assembly is the fabricated steel beam that physically bridges the two rear axles. Every tonne of rear axle load passes through this component on every kilometre driven. It is designed to be a life-of-vehicle structural component — not a scheduled replacement item. When it does need replacement, it is typically because of:

  • Fatigue cracking at weld zones, especially at the pivot eye weld toe
  • Structural deformation from overloading or severe impact on site roads
  • Pivot bore worn oversized by neglected bushing failure (metal-on-metal contact)

Failure Symptoms — What to Listen and Look For

Both bushing failure and cradle damage present with rear-end noise, but the character and circumstances differ:

SymptomLikely Cause
Clunking on rough terrain, reduces when emptyWorn bushings (22717828) — early/mid stage
Uneven rear tyre wear — one axle wears fasterWorn bushings — bogie beam tilted
Rubber extruding from pivot bush endsBushing rubber bond failed — replace immediately
2–3mm+ play at pivot with pry barWorn bushings or worn pivot bore (check bore diameter)
Noise persists after bushing replacementCradle crack or deformation (22913290)
One rear axle visibly higher than the otherCradle deformation — immediate investigation
Cracks visible at pivot eye or beam weldsCradle structural failure (22913290)
"Rocking horse" motion on flat groundCradle damage — axles no longer coordinated

Key diagnostic principle: If replacing the bogie bushings (22717828) does not eliminate the noise and handling issue, the investigation moves to the cradle assembly (22913290). Cracked or deformed beams produce structural movement that new bushings cannot correct.

The Decision: Bushings Only, or Full Cradle?

The most common mistake in bogie repairs is ordering the full cradle assembly when bushings alone are all that is needed — or conversely, fitting new bushings into a cradle with a worn-out pivot bore and wondering why the new bushings fail within weeks. Here is the decision framework:

Condition FoundAction
Cradle beam visually sound, pivot bore within specReplace bushings 22717828 only — do not replace cradle
Pivot bore slightly worn but within toleranceReplace bushings 22717828 — monitor at next service
Pivot bore oversized beyond bush press-fit specReplace cradle 22913290 AND new bushings 22717828
Crack found at pivot eye weldReplace cradle 22913290 — do not repair-weld the pivot eye
Visible beam deformation or bendReplace cradle 22913290
No visible damage but noise after bushing changeInspect for micro-cracks with dye penetrant

How to Inspect the Pivot Bore

When the cradle beam is removed for bushing replacement — which must be done in a hydraulic press — measure the pivot bore diameter with an inside micrometer or bore gauge. Compare to the bush outer sleeve diameter. The bush should require significant press force to fit — if it slides in by hand or with light mallet taps, the bore is oversized and the new bushings will not be retained correctly. At this point, the cradle assembly must be replaced.

For crack inspection, use dye penetrant testing: clean the weld zones, apply penetrant, allow 10–15 minutes dwell, wipe clean, apply developer. Any cracks will draw penetrant to the surface and appear as red indications. Focus on:

  • Pivot eye weld toes — the highest fatigue stress zone
  • Main beam flange welds — especially mid-span
  • Axle mounting bracket welds — check for movement-induced cracking

Replacement — What Parts to Order

Whether you are replacing bushings only or the full cradle, always replace the pivot fasteners at the same time. The pivot bolt (993079) and nut (984405) are torque-to-yield fasteners — they permanently deform during initial torqueing and must not be reused.

Bushing Replacement Kit

  • 22717828 × 2 — Bogie cradle bushings (LH + RH — always replace as a pair)
  • 993079 × 1 — Pivot bolt (replace every time)
  • 984405 × 1 — Pivot nut (replace every time)

Full Cradle Restoration Kit

  • 22913290 × 1 — Bogie cradle assembly
  • 22717828 × 2 — New bushings pressed into new cradle before fitment
  • 993079 × 1 — New pivot bolt
  • 984405 × 1 — New pivot nut
  • 1383063 — Bogie cover (inspect seal, replace if disturbed during assembly)

Never fit a new cradle with old bushings. The old bushings will have worn inner sleeves that create micro-play on the pivot bolt, and the rubber-to-metal bond will be fatigued. Within a short period, this play accelerates wear in the new cradle's pivot bore — destroying the new component you just installed.

Replacement Procedure Overview

Bushing Replacement (22717828) — Approx. 4 Hours

  1. Support chassis on heavy-duty stands, bogie fully unloaded. Jack under the cradle beam.
  2. Remove pivot bolt 993079 and nut 984405 — these are torque-to-yield, discard both.
  3. Lower cradle beam away from chassis pivot bracket.
  4. Press out worn bushings using hydraulic press (minimum 10-tonne) and correct adapters.
  5. Measure pivot bore diameter — confirm it is within specification before fitting new bushings.
  6. Apply light rubber lubricant to new bush outer sleeve. Press in squarely using hydraulic press. Bush must seat flush or just below the bore face. Never drive in with a hammer.
  7. Raise cradle back into pivot bracket. Fit new bolt and nut. Torque to Volvo WTM specification: ~200 Nm initial + 90° angle tighten.

Full Cradle Replacement (22913290) — Approx. 6–10 Hours

  1. Prepare workshop: hoist or pit, hydraulic press, torque wrench with angle gauge, Volvo WTM for your chassis VIN.
  2. Support vehicle, unload bogie. Remove pivot bolt and lower old cradle.
  3. Disconnect cradle from both rear axle mounting brackets per Volvo WTM procedure.
  4. Press new bushings 22717828 into new cradle pivot eye before fitting.
  5. Fit new cradle to both axle mounts, torque per Volvo WTM.
  6. Raise cradle into chassis pivot bracket. Fit new bolt 993079 and nut 984405, torque to specification.
  7. Verify geometry: both rear axles must be parallel and at equal height on flat ground before releasing vehicle.

Why Construction FMX Trucks Fail Faster

The bogie cradle bushing life figures of 300,000–500,000 km are based on on-highway operation within GVW limits. The reality for FMX500 trucks working construction sites in India, the Middle East, and Sub-Saharan Africa is frequently different:

  • Overloading: Construction sites routinely load trucks 10–15% beyond rated GVW. At the bogie pivot, this directly increases the torsional moment the bushing must resist — accelerating rubber fatigue proportionally.
  • Road surface: Sustained driving on boulder-strewn quarry tracks, deep ruts, and construction debris creates high-amplitude pivot cycling compared to highway use. A truck doing 200 km/day on quarry roads puts more bogie pivot load through the bushing than the same truck doing 500 km/day on highway.
  • Neglected inspection: Bogie bushings are out of sight under the vehicle. Without a structured inspection regime, worn bushings continue in service until metal-on-metal contact develops — at which point the pivot bore may already be damaged, turning a bushing job into a full cradle replacement.

The practical recommendation: inspect bogie bushings visually at every major service (every 60,000–80,000 km for construction use) and physically measure pivot play at every bushing replacement regardless of mileage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the bogie cradle on the Volvo FMX500?

The bogie cradle (part 22913290) is the fabricated steel beam connecting both tandem rear drive axles on the Volvo FMX 6×4. It pivots on a central chassis bracket, allowing each axle to articulate independently over rough terrain while distributing load equally.

What is the bogie cradle bushing 22717828?

Part 22717828 is the rubber-bonded steel bushing in the pivot eye of the cradle beam. It allows controlled cradle rotation while absorbing vibration and load. It is a wear item — replace in pairs (LH + RH) at approximately 300,000 km intervals in normal use, or sooner in heavy construction operation.

How do I tell the difference between a worn bushing and a damaged cradle?

Worn bushings (22717828): clunking over rough terrain, visible rubber cracking, 2–3mm+ pry-bar play at pivot, uneven tyre wear. These improve after bushing replacement. Damaged cradle (22913290): symptoms that persist after bushing replacement, visible cracking at weld zones, one axle higher than the other, structural noise from the beam itself. Use dye penetrant to inspect welds definitively.

What parts are needed for a complete bogie restoration?

22913290 (cradle, if structurally damaged) + 22717828 × 2 (new bushings) + 993079 (new pivot bolt) + 984405 (new pivot nut). Inspect bogie cover 1383063 and replace if disturbed.
