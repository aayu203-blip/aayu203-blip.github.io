Why Renault and Volvo Share This Part

2001

Volvo Group Acquires Renault Trucks

Volvo AB acquires 100% of Renault Trucks (then called Renault V.I.) from the Renault S.A. group. From this point, engineering development for heavy truck platforms begins to be consolidated across both brands.

2002–2010

Platform Sharing Begins

Volvo Group engineering rationalises components shared between Volvo and Renault Trucks. Front suspension elements — including the hollow spring rubber buffer — are among the first platform components standardised. The same part, same specification, same part number appears in both the Volvo EPC and the Renault Trucks EPC.

Today

Common Part, Two Brands

Part 20390836 (formerly 1629553) appears in both Volvo and Renault Trucks parts catalogues as the current active number for the front suspension hollow spring. A single production run supplies both brands through the Volvo Group supply chain.

Renault Premiums fitted with front air suspension (a factory option on some long-haul variants) use a different suspension geometry and do not use this part. If you are unsure, check the front axle — leaf spring variants will have a visible spring pack running longitudinally under the axle.

Why Renault Parts Are More Expensive Through Renault Channels

Genuine Renault-branded parts sourced through Renault dealer networks in India carry a significant price premium over the same component ordered through Volvo aftermarket channels. There are a few reasons for this:

  • Smaller distribution volume — Renault Trucks has a smaller installed base in India than Volvo, meaning lower local distribution volumes and higher per-unit channel costs.
  • Dealer margin structure — authorised Renault Trucks dealers carry higher parts margins than independent suppliers.
  • Same supply chain, different packaging — in many cases, the Renault-branded part and the Volvo-branded part come from the same production batch. The price difference is purely channel cost.
