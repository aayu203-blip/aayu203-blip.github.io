The Problem with Multi-Supplier Fleet Management

Managing a heavy vehicle fleet across the Gulf means dealing with a fundamental parts problem: your fleet runs multiple brands, but most parts suppliers specialise in one. A fleet with Scania R580 prime movers, Volvo FH16 tippers, and CAT 777G haul trucks would typically need three separate supplier relationships — three payment cycles, three lead times, three sets of quality checks, three logistics chains.

For a Dubai-based regional logistics group managing over 200 heavy vehicles across UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Oman, this fragmentation had become operationally costly. Parts arrived in separate shipments on different days. Invoicing was a maze. Quality was inconsistent — especially for the Scania drivetrain components, where sourcing had come from a rotating cast of Indian and Chinese traders.

What the Client Required

The group's procurement head reached out to PTC with a specific brief:

  • A single, verified supplier for Scania, Volvo, and CAT parts
  • Monthly consolidated shipments — one container, pre-sorted by fleet and vehicle registration
  • Consistent parts quality with clear OEM vs. aftermarket labelling on every item
  • Direct technical support on the phone for part number verification — not just a catalogue

The requirement list covered everything from Scania R-series clutch kits and engine seals to Volvo FH16 alternators and injectors to CAT 777G brake pads and hydraulic filters.

How the Supply Agreement Works

PTC and the client established a monthly consolidated shipment schedule after a three-month trial period. The structure:

  • Rolling order sheet: The client's workshop managers submit requirements by the 20th of each month via a shared WhatsApp group.
  • Consolidated proforma within 24 hours: PTC issues a single invoice covering all items, all brands.
  • Pre-sorted packaging: Each shipment arrives with parts grouped and labelled by vehicle fleet — Scania, Volvo, CAT — so the warehouse team can distribute without sorting.
  • Air freight for urgent items: Critical failures between monthly cycles are handled as standalone urgent orders, typically dispatched within 24 hours.

Five Years Later

The client has not used a secondary parts supplier in over five years. Three different procurement managers have rotated through the role in that time — the supply agreement has outlasted all of them, continuing through handovers without interruption.

The consistency of supply has allowed the group's workshop to move from reactive to planned maintenance, with known parts availability for scheduled service intervals. Lead time predictability alone has reduced unplanned downtime significantly compared to the spot-buying approach.
