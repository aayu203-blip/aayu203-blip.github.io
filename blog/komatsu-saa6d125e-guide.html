Komatsu SAA6D125E Engine Parts Guide

OEM part numbers, service intervals, overhaul criteria, and India-specific maintenance notes for the SAA6D125E-3 and SAA6D125E-5 — covering PC400, PC450, D155, GD675, and WA470/500 applications.

By Vinesh Shah — Parts Trading Company · May 2026

SAA6D125E Family Overview

The SAA6D125E is Komatsu's workhorse large-displacement engine, used across the widest range of heavy equipment in the Komatsu lineup. It is a 6-cylinder inline diesel with 11.0-litre displacement — bore 125 mm × stroke 150 mm — producing between 259 and 276 kW (347–370 hp) depending on variant and rating. The engine has been in continuous production and development since the late 1990s, and the parts base is mature and well-supported in India.

Two variants are commonly encountered in the Indian market:

  • SAA6D125E-3 (Tier 2 / Stage II) — Meets less stringent emissions standards. Common on PC400-7, D155A-6, and GD675-3 machines built before 2012. No DPF or EGR; mechanically straightforward to service.
  • SAA6D125E-5 (Tier 4 Final / Stage IV) — Introduces EGR and a diesel particulate filter (DPF). Used in PC400-8M0, PC450-8, D155A-7, WA470-6, and WA500-6. Higher service complexity but same core architecture.

Applications where you will find this engine: PC400-7, PC400-8, PC450-7, PC450-8 (large hydraulic excavators); D155A-6, D155A-7 (large crawler dozers); GD675-3 (motor grader); WA470-6, WA500-6 (wheel loaders). The engine's 11-litre displacement and modest rated speed (1800 rpm) give it a reputation for longevity — well-maintained examples routinely reach 15,000–20,000 hours before overhaul is required.

Fuel Injection Pump (Bosch PE6A)

The SAA6D125E uses a Bosch PE6A inline injection pump — a proven, mechanically robust unit that has been in production for decades. The OEM Komatsu part number is 6251-71-1120. The Bosch reman equivalent (0-460-424-196) is widely accepted and typically arrives pre-calibrated to OEM specification; it costs approximately 30–40% less than a new OEM pump and carries a manufacturer warranty.

Timing specification is 2° BTDC static at idle. Incorrect timing is the most common cause of injection pump-related complaints. The diagnostic logic is straightforward: hard start combined with black smoke indicates retarded pump timing — the fuel is being delivered late, combustion is incomplete. White smoke combined with excessive fuel consumption points to worn plungers — the pump is delivering fuel erratically, with incomplete atomisation at low pressure.

Before condemning the injection pump, verify the fuel supply side. A blocked fuel filter or a failing lift pump can cause symptoms identical to pump wear. Check fuel pressure at the pump inlet — minimum 0.15 bar at cranking speed. If fuel supply is confirmed good and timing is correct, proceed to plunger clearance measurement. Plunger-to-barrel clearance above 0.006 mm at rated conditions indicates replacement is due.

Liner and Piston Overhaul Criteria

The SAA6D125E uses wet-type cylinder liners with a nominal bore of 125 mm. Liner condition assessment is the critical first step in any overhaul decision. Measure liner wear using a bore gauge at three points from the top of the liner: 10 mm, 50 mm, and 120 mm below the top face. The wear limit is 0.30 mm from nominal bore — beyond this, oil consumption rises sharply and ring sealing deteriorates.

Liner protrusion above the block deck must be checked before reassembly: the acceptable range is 0.02–0.10 mm. Insufficient protrusion causes head gasket leakage; excessive protrusion risks cracking the liner flange under head bolt torque.

For the pistons, the piston pin clearance wear limit is 0.15 mm. Top ring groove wear limit is 0.35 mm — measured with the ring installed in the groove, check side clearance with a feeler gauge. New ring side clearance is 0.09–0.13 mm; any groove exceeding 0.35 mm total width requires a new piston.

Torque specifications for reassembly: main bearing caps 235 Nm + 90° (torque-to-yield); connecting rod bolts 98 Nm + 90°. These are torque-to-yield fasteners — replace them at every overhaul without exception. Reusing stretched TTY fasteners is one of the leading causes of repeat bearing failures on this engine.

Common Issues in Indian Mining Conditions

The SAA6D125E has a solid reliability record globally, but Indian mining and quarry operations expose it to conditions that exceed the design assumptions of the factory maintenance schedule. These are the failure modes we see most frequently at PTC:

  • Injector tip erosion from dust contamination: Quarry dust that bypasses a damaged or infrequently serviced air filter reaches the fuel system via the intake-to-combustion path. Even microscopic abrasives in the fuel attack injector tip orifices, widening them over time and causing uneven fuel distribution across cylinders. In active quarry operations, change fuel filters every 250 hr rather than the factory 500 hr, and inspect air filter condition every 100 hr. A blocked air filter creates intake vacuum that can pull contaminated air past seals.
  • Air filter choking: In active quarries and coal mines, the outer air filter element can reach its restriction limit well before 500 hr. Install a pre-cleaner (cyclonic or centrifugal type) at the air intake to extend filter life. Monitor the restriction indicator on the air cleaner housing at every fuel fill.
  • Thermostat failure in summer: Indian summer ambient temperatures in central and western India regularly exceed 45°C. At these ambient temperatures, a thermostat that opens sluggishly or at the wrong temperature causes coolant temperature to spike during high-load operation. Replace the thermostat (600-421-6110) every 2,000 hr as standard practice in machines operating in Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, and similar high-temperature regions.
  • EGR cooler soot buildup (E-5 only): On SAA6D125E-5 equipped machines, the EGR cooler accumulates soot at a rate proportional to engine load factor. At Indian mining load factors (typically 80–90%), clean the EGR cooler every 2,000 hr. Deferred cleaning leads to EGR flow restriction, increased combustion temperatures, and accelerated piston crown erosion.

Cross-References and Interchangeability

The SAA6D125E-3 and SAA6D125E-5 share the vast majority of internal engine components — block, head, crank, con-rods, liners, pistons, rings, and valve train are common across variants. The key differences are in the fuel injection system (different pump and injector specifications on E-5) and all emissions-related components (EGR system, DPF, sensors). When ordering parts, always verify with the machine serial number to confirm exact variant.

Many SAA6D125E internal parts also interchange with the D155A-7 dozer and GD675-3 grader — both use this engine family. This cross-application parts commonality is a significant advantage for fleet operators running mixed Komatsu equipment: a single overhaul kit covers multiple machine types.
