What Is a Volvo Rubber Spring?

A rubber spring (also called a suspension buffer or elephant foot in workshop manuals) is a load-bearing rubber block mounted between the front axle and the chassis frame on Volvo FH, FM, and FMX trucks. Its job is simple: absorb the vertical shock when the front axle hits a bump, damp out vibration before it reaches the cab, and prevent the steel axle from making direct contact with the steel chassis rail.

These parts work under constant compression. On a loaded long-haul truck or tipper, the rubber is under significant stress every single kilometre. They eventually harden, crack, and lose their elasticity — at which point the truck starts shaking more, tyres wear unevenly, and the chassis takes stress it wasn't designed to absorb.

When to replace: Inspect at every 50,000 km service. Replace when you see cracking, permanent deformation, or when the driver reports increased vibration or handling changes. Always replace as a pair — fitting one new and one worn creates uneven axle geometry.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Volvo rubber spring?
A rubber spring is a front axle suspension buffer that absorbs road shock between the axle and chassis on Volvo FH, FM, and FMX trucks. It prevents metal-to-metal contact, reduces vibration, and helps maintain stable front axle geometry under load.
What is part number 20390836 and what does it supersede?
Part 20390836 is the current Volvo part number for the front axle rubber spring. It directly supersedes 1629553 — both refer to the same component. The number 7420390836 is the same part with a market prefix. All three cross-reference identically.
How often should I replace Volvo rubber springs?
Inspect at every 50,000 km service. Replace when you see visible cracking, permanent compression, or when the truck shows increased front-end vibration or uneven tyre wear. Replace as a pair — not one side at a time.
