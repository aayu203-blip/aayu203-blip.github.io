What Is a Hollow Spring / Rubber Buffer Mount?

Technical overview of part 20390836 and its function in heavy vehicle suspension

Function & Design

The hollow spring — correctly termed a rubber buffer mount or suspension spring arm mounting bush — is a cylindrical vibration isolation element positioned between the front axle assembly and the vehicle frame in leaf-spring-equipped heavy trucks.

Its hollow geometry is intentional: the empty core provides a progressive spring rate. Under small deflections (normal road inputs), the rubber acts as a soft damper. As compression increases under hard impacts or heavy load, the walls contact the central bore and effective spring rate increases sharply — protecting the chassis from metal-to-metal contact.

The steel plate vulcanisation-bonded into each end transmits clamping load evenly into the rubber compound, preventing localised stress concentrations and delamination under sustained cyclic loading.

Why It Matters for Fleet Operators

A failed hollow spring is frequently misdiagnosed as a wheel balance issue, tyre defect, or shock absorber fault. The actual symptoms — cab vibration, front-end noise, asymmetric tyre wear — are identical, leading to unnecessary repair costs.

The part is inexpensive relative to the secondary damage it prevents. A degraded rubber mount allows micro-movement in the spring mounting stack, which gradually elongates U-bolt holes in the axle pad, destroys leaf spring eye bushings, and accelerates hub bearing wear through abnormal axle geometry.

  • Typical OEM service life: 300,000–500,000 km
  • Replace in pairs — asymmetric wear causes handling imbalance
  • Check spring seats at replacement — corrosion causes premature new-part failure
  • Also known as: hollow spring, rubber buffer, jounce buffer, suspension bush, spring mount rubber

Signs of a Failed Hollow Spring / Rubber Buffer

Diagnostic reference for mechanics, fleet managers, and workshop supervisors — part 20390836

Early Stage

Persistent Cab Vibration at Highway Speed

Rubber hardens over time, losing its damping coefficient. Sustained vibration felt in the steering wheel and cab floor between 60–90 km/h. Frequently misattributed to tyre imbalance — check the hollow spring first.

Early–Mid Stage

Audible Clunking Over Potholes

As the hollow core closes due to permanent compression set, metal-to-metal contact occurs inside the mounting stack. Produces a dull, low-frequency clunk — distinct from shock absorber noise, more of a thud than a rattle.

Mid Stage

Asymmetric Front Tyre Wear

One buffer degrading ahead of the other creates camber asymmetry on the front axle. Inner-edge or outer-edge wear on one front tyre — not correctable by wheel alignment alone — is a key indicator.

Mid Stage

Vehicle Pulling Under Heavy Braking

Unequal rubber stiffness left vs. right manifests as directional pull under braking. If the brake system checks clean but the pull persists, asymmetric suspension geometry is the cause.

Mid Stage

Uneven Load Transfer and Suspension Instability

Degraded hollow springs allow excess chassis pitch and roll under load. Particularly noticeable when cornering under load or on uneven terrain — the vehicle feels "loose" in the front end.

Mid–Late Stage

Visible Rubber Cracking or Bulging

Surface cracks (fine = early, deep = replace now), lateral bulging indicating internal delamination, or a noticeably flattened height compared to the opposite side. Inspect from below with a torch.

Late Stage

Cracked Rubber Layers / Metal Separation

Delamination at the steel-rubber interface, particularly at outer edges. Common in vehicles operating in dust, chemical exposure, or extreme thermal cycling — mining, quarry, and port applications.

Late Stage — Replace Immediately

Steel Insert Exposure or Separation

The vulcanised bond between rubber and steel fails under sustained cyclic overload. Steel plate becomes visible through the rubber or moves independently. No remaining isolation capacity. Replace immediately — secondary suspension damage accelerates rapidly.

Secondary Damage Indicator

Accelerated Hub Bearing Wear

A failed hollow spring allows micro-movement in the spring mounting stack. Over time, incorrect axle geometry places asymmetric load on front wheel bearings, dramatically shortening their service life.

Frequently Asked Questions

Technical and procurement questions about part 20390836 / hollow spring rubber buffer

What is the Volvo 20390836 hollow spring?
Part 20390836 is a hollow spring rubber buffer mount — a cylindrical rubber-and-steel vibration isolation assembly used in the front suspension of Volvo FH, FM, FMX, F7/F10/F12, and Renault truck families. The hollow core gives it a progressive spring rate: soft on small vibrations, stiff under heavy impact. Also listed as 1629553 (superseded) and 7420390836.
Is 1629553 the same as 20390836?
Yes — identical part. Volvo superseded the old number 1629553 with the current number 20390836. If you have the old number on your existing spring or in a legacy parts list, order 20390836. You will receive exactly the same component.
Why is it called a "hollow spring"?
The term "hollow spring" describes the part's geometry — a rubber cylinder with a hollow bore through the centre, which gives it spring-like progressive compression. The formal technical names are rubber buffer mount, jounce buffer, or suspension spring arm mounting bush. "Hollow spring" is the common workshop term used in India, UK, and Scandinavia.
What are the approximate dimensions of 20390836?
Outer diameter: ~90 mm. Inner bore: ~36–40 mm. Free height: ~70 mm. Steel plate thickness: ~3–4 mm. These are representative values — measure your existing part with a calibrated caliper for precision procurement. PTC can provide a sample for dimensional approval on bulk orders.
What Shore A hardness is the rubber compound?
OEM-specification: approximately 55–65° Shore A. This provides the correct balance of vibration damping and load capacity. Low-grade copies often use 70–80° Shore A rubber, which significantly reduces damping effectiveness and accelerates secondary wear on surrounding components.
What temperatures can part 20390836 withstand?
The natural rubber compound is rated for −40°C to +120°C continuous operating temperature. This covers all heavy commercial vehicle operating environments from cold-weather logistics to tropical construction. Exposure to petroleum-based fluids (fuel, hydraulic oil) can degrade natural rubber — check for leaks in the spring area when replacing.
How do I inspect the hollow spring without removing it?
With the truck on level ground, use a torch and mirror to view the rubber buffer from below the front axle. Check for: surface cracking (fine = early stage, deep = replace now), lateral bulging, height asymmetry left vs. right, and any exposed steel insert. A quick functional test: with the engine off, have an assistant rock the steering wheel while you watch for movement in the rubber mount stack.
Must I replace both sides at the same time?
Yes — always replace in pairs. Both sides have accumulated identical mileage and load cycles. Replacing one side creates a stiffness asymmetry between left and right front suspension, causing the same cab vibration, tyre wear, and handling imbalance you were trying to fix. The cost of the second piece is minimal vs. the workshop time saved.
What is the difference between OEM and aftermarket rubber buffer?
OEM (genuine Volvo) and OEM-specification aftermarket (what PTC supplies) are manufactured to the same dimensional tolerances and rubber compound grade. Low-quality aftermarket copies use inferior compounds with incorrect hardness, reduced temperature range, and poor steel bonding — failing significantly earlier.
